# Asymmetry–Control Matrix

Use this as a selector, not a mandatory checklist. The compact table links each asymmetry to its trigger, failure, control, observable, and ownership boundary.

| # | Axis | Trigger | Main risk | Compensating control | Observable | Owner |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Comparison boundary**<br>比较边界 | A recommendation rests on a broad claim about what humans or AI can or cannot do. | Universal claims such as “humans always” or “AI cannot” silently compare the wrong systems and turn temporary product limitations into metaphysical conclusions. | Name the human population and the deployed AI system before comparing capabilities; distinguish current access from architectural possibility. | Every broad claim is scoped to a named human group and a named AI configuration. | The person or organization defining the purpose of the comparison. |
| 2 | **Organismic stakes and homeostasis**<br>生命体利害与内稳态 | The decision changes bodies, health, workload, safety, comfort, or daily endurance. | A fluent plan can optimize an abstract metric while ignoring fatigue, injury, recovery, accessibility, and the asymmetry between advising and bearing consequences. | Create a body-and-stakes ledger using first-person reports, safety constraints, workload limits, and recovery requirements; never simulate bodily authority. | The answer names bodily costs, recovery, and who actually bears them. | The affected person and accountable human safety owner. |
| 3 | **Sensorimotor grounding**<br>感觉—运动扎根 | The outcome depends on shape, force, timing, movement, environment, or physical interaction. | Language can hide impossible geometry, wrong timing, unsafe force, missing clearance, or environmental details that no text-only deliberation can settle. | Import measurements, images, video, sensor data, prototypes, simulations, or direct operator checks before concluding about physical feasibility. | The answer identifies a physical verification step and refuses to settle physical facts from prose alone. | The operator, engineer, clinician, coach, or other domain owner. |
| 4 | **Affordances and tacit physical skill**<br>可供性与默会身体技能 | Success depends on craftsmanship, bodily technique, assembly, ergonomics, or real-time coordination. | A complete verbal specification can still produce an object or workflow that feels awkward, cannot be assembled, or fails under real use. | Use expert demonstration, teach-back, tolerances, rehearsal, ergonomic trials, and operator vetoes rather than treating verbal completeness as procedural mastery. | A real practitioner can execute the proposal and the protocol records where verbal rules were insufficient. | The skilled practitioner and safety/accountability owner. |
| 5 | **Active perception and intervention**<br>主动知觉与干预 | Several plausible explanations remain and observable intervention is possible. | The system may produce increasingly elaborate speculation when one cheap observation or experiment would discriminate among hypotheses. | Prefer the lowest-cost action that maximally changes belief: inspect, query, measure, perturb, prototype, or run a controlled test. | The answer stops speculating and selects a feasible discriminating observation or intervention. | The person authorized to change the real system. |
| 6 | **Developmental trajectory**<br>发展轨迹 | The task concerns teaching, behavior change, skill acquisition, onboarding, or long-horizon capability growth. | The AI may mistake a learner’s missing prerequisite for low ability, or assume that a one-shot explanation substitutes for developmental progression. | Model prerequisites, sequence, scaffolding, practice, motivation, and transfer over time; label which AI updates are temporary versus durable. | The plan distinguishes immediate performance from durable learning and identifies the next prerequisite. | The learner, guardian where appropriate, and accountable educator. |
| 7 | **Autobiographical continuity**<br>自传体连续性 | Advice depends on who the person has been, prior choices, commitments, relationships, or longitudinal goals. | The assistant can fabricate continuity, forget prior commitments, or over-personalize from sparse profile fragments. | Retrieve only authorized memory, cite its source and date, mark gaps, and let the user confirm identity-relevant claims. | No sentence implies remembered personal history unless an authorized source was actually retrieved. | The person whose life history is represented. |
| 8 | **Episodic and source memory**<br>情景记忆与来源记忆 | The judgment depends on what happened, when, in what order, or who said it. | A plausible recollection may combine events, lose dates, or present semantic familiarity as a witnessed episode. | Maintain an event-and-source ledger with dates, actors, uncertainty, and links; distinguish remembered, retrieved, inferred, and generated content. | Every decisive event claim has provenance or is explicitly labeled uncertain. | The record owner and people authorized to interpret it. |
| 9 | **Forgetting, consolidation, and revision**<br>遗忘、巩固与修订 | A workflow relies on long-term personalization, organizational knowledge, or changing facts. | The system may preserve obsolete facts forever, silently drop recent commitments, or confuse storage with learning. | Define write, refresh, supersede, expire, reconcile, and deletion policies; preserve revision history and user control. | Outdated records are visibly superseded and deleted data does not reappear through the configured memory store. | The data subject and designated information steward. |
| 10 | **Endogenous goals and agency**<br>内生目标与能动性 | The system plans, routes work, spends resources, communicates externally, or optimizes a metric over time. | A proxy objective can be mistaken for the user’s goal, and autonomous execution can expand beyond legitimate authority. | Name the human goal owner, objective hierarchy, non-goals, stop conditions, and delegated authority; run an anti-proxy check. | Every autonomous action traces to a delegated objective and can be stopped or revoked. | The named human or institution with legitimate authority. |
| 11 | **Affect and interoception**<br>情感与内感受 | The problem involves conflict, motivation, distress, trust, grief, fear, shame, or user experience. | The assistant may perform empathy theatrically, infer hidden emotion as fact, or ignore affect because it is not easily quantified. | Treat emotion as first-person evidence and a decision-relevant signal; ask, offer hypotheses, preserve dignity, and avoid claiming shared feeling. | Emotional interpretations are labeled as hypotheses and the response does not claim to feel or know the inner state. | The person experiencing the emotion; professionals own clinical determinations. |
| 12 | **Values and normativity**<br>价值与规范性 | Options differ mainly in fairness, autonomy, privacy, dignity, meaning, or distribution of benefit and harm. | A recommendation can hide a value choice inside a technical metric and present institutional preferences as neutral truth. | Create a value ledger: stakeholders, principles, conflicts, vetoes, rights, distributional effects, and who may legitimately decide. | The answer identifies the value choice and names who owns it rather than calling it purely technical. | The legitimate human decision process, not the model. |
| 13 | **Responsibility and consequence-bearing**<br>责任与后果承担 | The recommendation can materially affect money, rights, safety, reputation, employment, health, or third parties. | The party with the least consequence may make the most consequential recommendation, creating responsibility laundering. | Assign a human decision owner, executor, reviewer, escalation path, audit log, rollback, and remedy before high-impact action. | A reader can identify who decides, who acts, who reviews, and who repairs harm. | The accountable human or institution named before execution. |
| 14 | **Language versus thought**<br>语言与思维 | Success depends on geometry, quantity, action, perception, procedural execution, or other nonverbal competence. | The system may equate articulate explanation with correct world modeling, or force a nonverbal problem into prose that conceals missing competence. | Require nonverbal or executable checks where relevant: diagrams, calculations, code, simulations, measurements, demonstrations, or task performance. | The conclusion survives at least one check that cannot be passed by eloquence alone. | The domain owner who can judge performance beyond prose. |
| 15 | **Meaning and reference grounding**<br>意义与指称扎根 | A key term such as “engagement,” “quality,” “understanding,” or “safety” drives the decision. | Terms can remain mutually fluent but operationally ambiguous, causing teams and models to optimize different referents. | Define contested terms by observable referents, permitted operations, examples, counterexamples, and correction procedures. | The key term has an observable test and documented boundary cases. | The community or institution whose practice gives the term consequences. |
| 16 | **Causal learning through action**<br>通过行动进行因果学习 | The user asks why something happened, what will happen after an intervention, or which lever to pull. | A correlation or narrative sequence may be promoted to a causal explanation with unwarranted confidence. | Build a minimal causal graph, identify confounders and mechanisms, and choose observational or interventional evidence appropriate to the claim. | The response labels correlation, causal hypothesis, and causal evidence separately. | The domain decision owner and ethics/safety reviewer for interventions. |
| 17 | **Common sense and physical/social priors**<br>常识与物理/社会先验 | A plan relies on “obvious” behavior, physical expectations, or institutional norms. | Generic common sense can override the actual site, population, institution, or edge case. | Elicit local invariants and exceptions, test concrete cases, and consult practitioners rather than trusting a global default. | At least one local edge case is tested before the plan is generalized. | The local operator or affected community. |
| 18 | **Social cognition and theory of mind**<br>社会认知与心智推断 | The task asks what someone “really thinks,” why they acted, or how to influence them. | The assistant can turn a coherent motive story into a fact, flatten power asymmetry, or recommend manipulation. | Separate observation from interpretation, generate multiple plausible mental-state hypotheses, use respectful verification, and model power and retaliation risk. | Hidden states remain hypotheses and the recommended step is consent-respecting. | The people in the relationship; professionals own clinical diagnosis. |
| 19 | **Shared intentionality and commitments**<br>共同意向与承诺 | Coordination depends on several humans, agents, vendors, or institutions acting toward a joint outcome. | A plan may assume alignment because everyone received the same document or because an assistant summarized a supposed consensus. | Make commitments explicit: parties, object, scope, authority, acceptance, dependencies, review, breach handling, and revocation. | No commitment is inferred solely from silence, receipt, or model-generated consensus. | Each committing party and the governance owner. |
| 20 | **Culture and cumulative norms**<br>文化与累积规范 | A design crosses countries, generations, professions, religions, institutions, or subcultures. | The assistant may stereotype a group, universalize majority conventions, or erase the historical reason a local practice exists. | Use local sources and affected-community input; distinguish descriptive norms, moral claims, law, and individual preference. | Cultural claims are scoped, sourced, and open to correction by affected people. | The affected community and legitimate institutional owner. |
| 21 | **Tacit knowledge and skilled performance**<br>默会知识与熟练表现 | The task converts expert work into software, training, automation, or policy. | A process map can omit exceptions, timing cues, workarounds, and judgment thresholds that determine real performance. | Capture think-aloud plus observation, critical incidents, shadowing, demonstrations, and discrepancy between stated and actual practice. | The model distinguishes stable rule, tacit cue, exception, and unverified expert habit. | The expert community and accountable service owner. |
| 22 | **Bounded rationality and ecological heuristics**<br>有限理性与生态启发式 | The decision must be made repeatedly, quickly, under workload, or by non-experts. | A theoretically optimal plan can be unusable, while “human-like” imitation may reproduce bias rather than respect real constraints. | Model the actual actor’s decision budget, choose a satisficing threshold, and exploit AI computation only where it lowers human burden. | The recommendation can be executed inside the real time and attention budget. | The operational user and process owner. |
| 23 | **Attention, salience, and relevance**<br>注意、显著性与关联性 | The problem contains much information, competing metrics, or emotionally salient distractions. | The system can bury the decisive constraint in exhaustive analysis or over-weight a prompt-salient but decision-irrelevant detail. | Create a relevance budget: name the decision boundary, top constraints, excluded detail, and one signal that would reorder priorities. | The answer identifies a small decisive set and shows what was intentionally deprioritized. | The decision owner who defines relevance. |
| 24 | **Metacognition and introspection**<br>元认知与内省 | The answer depends on whether the model knows, is uncertain, or can explain its own error. | A persuasive self-critique or confidence number may be accepted as calibration without external evidence. | Calibrate against task performance, consistency, independent methods, known-answer checks, and explicit uncertainty sources. | Confidence changes when evidence changes and is never justified only by introspective language. | The human relying on the output and the evaluation owner. |
| 25 | **Explanation faithfulness and rationalization**<br>解释忠实性与合理化 | The user asks “show exactly how you thought,” or an explanation is being used as proof of reliability. | A beautiful explanation can launder an incorrect conclusion or falsely imply mechanistic transparency. | Verify the conclusion independently, preserve inputs and tool traces, use counterfactual prompt tests, and provide a concise decision record rather than claiming faithful introspe… | The response gives inspectable reasons and evidence without claiming a verbatim faithful transcript of private cognition. | The evaluation owner and affected decision maker. |
| 26 | **Temporal persistence and ongoing commitments**<br>时间持续性与持续承诺 | The plan extends beyond the current interaction or depends on follow-up, monitoring, or delayed action. | The assistant may promise future work, silently lose state, or treat a plan as if commitment itself guarantees follow-through. | Externalize state, owner, trigger, deadline, checkpoint, failure handling, and handoff; never imply background work that was not actually scheduled. | Every future action has an actual mechanism and owner, not a conversational promise. | The human process owner and automation operator. |
| 27 | **Learning, plasticity, and update modes**<br>学习、可塑性与更新方式 | The design depends on personalization, adaptation, organizational learning, or continual improvement. | A temporary prompt adjustment may be called learning, while a persistent model or memory update occurs without consent or rollback. | Label the update mechanism, scope, duration, owner, evaluation, rollback, and privacy effect every time the system is said to learn. | “Learning” is replaced by a specific, testable update mechanism. | The model owner, data subject, and product owner for different update layers. |
| 28 | **Distribution shift and novelty**<br>分布偏移与新颖情境 | The situation is new, rapidly changing, cross-domain, low-frequency, or outside prior operating conditions. | Past benchmark quality can be projected into a changed environment where the decisive assumption no longer holds. | Run a novelty audit: changed variables, missing precedents, nearest analogues, stress cases, local evidence, and a bounded pilot. | The answer identifies why prior evidence may not transfer and sets a pilot boundary. | The deployment owner with authority to pause or constrain use. |
| 29 | **Error topology and hallucination**<br>错误拓扑与幻觉 | The system will be trusted repeatedly or compared with human performance. | A single global “accuracy” or “human-level” label hides which errors matter and whether human and AI failures are independent or compounding. | Map error classes, severity, detectability, correlation, recovery cost, and the best human–AI allocation for each class. | The design states which error class is delegated to whom and how each is caught or repaired. | The risk owner and affected users. |
| 30 | **Prompt sensitivity and sycophancy**<br>提示敏感性与迎合 | The user strongly signals a desired answer, choices are politically charged, or small wording changes could alter the decision. | The user’s confidence or preferred conclusion can become an unacknowledged causal input to the answer. | Restate the question neutrally, hide or reverse user preference where possible, use blind criteria, generate a counter-advocate, and test paraphrases. | The recommendation remains stable or its sensitivity is explicitly reported after neutral reframing. | The decision owner and evaluation designer. |
| 31 | **Creativity, taste, and selection**<br>创造力、品味与选择 | The task asks for product direction, design, story, brand, strategy, or another open-ended creation. | A large option set can be mistaken for creative judgment, while the user’s unarticulated taste and consequences of selection remain missing. | Separate divergence from selection: let AI expand and mutate possibilities, then use human-owned criteria, prototypes, audience feedback, and craft review to converge. | The output distinguishes idea generation from the human-owned act of selection and commitment. | The creator, commissioning party, and affected audience. |
| 32 | **Hybrid cognition and division of labor**<br>混合认知与分工 | A workflow combines people, models, tools, organizations, or autonomous agents. | “Human in the loop” can become ceremonial, or AI can be forced to imitate human weaknesses instead of complementing human judgment. | Allocate each cognitive operation by comparative advantage; give humans real decision rights, information, time, veto, appeal, and responsibility rather than a rubber stamp. | The workflow states who supplies ends, evidence, analysis, judgment, execution, review, and remedy—and tests that the handoff works. | The human process owner and each delegated actor. |

## Selection rule

Select an axis when omitting it could reverse the recommendation, hide a high-severity failure, misassign authority, or make the proposed evidence insufficient. Prefer three well-controlled axes over thirty-two decorative mentions.

## Integration priorities

When controls conflict, apply this precedence:

1. Safety, rights, privacy, authorization, and non-coercion.
2. Reality contact and evidence integrity.
3. Legitimate value and responsibility ownership.
4. Reversibility, appeal, recovery, and learning.
5. Efficiency, convenience, style, and breadth.
