# Research Synthesis: What “Human–AI Difference” Means Here

## Status of the question

There is no single, settled scientific theorem that states the metaphysical essence of “human thought” and “AI thought.” Human cognition is heterogeneous, AI systems are changing, and similar behavior can arise from different mechanisms. This Skill therefore uses a conservative **operational synthesis**, not a consciousness test or a declaration that one architecture can never acquire a capability.

## The synthesis

### 1. Human cognition is organismic and situated

Human decisions occur in a living body with safety, fatigue, pain, affect, development, mortality, relationships, and material consequences. Grounded-cognition research emphasizes that perception, action, bodily states, and situations can participate in conceptual processing. The Skill operationalizes this by requiring a body-and-stakes ledger, concrete episode, affected-person input, and physical verification where relevant.

### 2. Current AI behavior is system-relative

An AI product is not merely “the model.” Behavior depends on model, context, modalities, retrieval, tools, memory, sensors, actuators, permissions, policies, automation, and operators. A limitation of a text-only turn should not become “AI can never,” while a robot’s sensors should not become evidence of human-like feeling. The runtime inventory prevents both errors.

### 3. Language performance is not the whole of human thought

Human research supports meaningful dissociations between language and other cognitive functions. LLM research also distinguishes formal linguistic competence from broader functional competence. The Skill therefore rejects eloquence as proof of world modeling and demands executable, perceptual, causal, or outcome-based checks when the task is not fundamentally verbal.

### 4. Meaning, causality, and common sense require correction by use and world contact

Human concepts are shaped through action, correction, shared practices, causal interventions, and local institutions. A model can represent powerful statistical regularities, but a decision should import current sources, observations, measurements, demonstrations, experiments, and affected-community correction rather than relying on internal coherence alone.

### 5. Human memory and agency are not equivalent to context and optimization

Human autobiographical and episodic memory supports identity, commitments, and prospective simulation, while AI systems divide state across context, external stores, retrieval, and model updates. Similarly, agentic execution under a supplied objective does not establish self-owned goals. The Skill names the goal owner, memory mechanism, persistence, consent, and revocation.

### 6. Values, shared intentions, and responsibility are socially constituted

Human groups create norms, commitments, authority, sanctions, appeal, and cumulative culture. AI can model and document these structures, but generated agreement is not consent and execution is not legitimate authority. The Skill keeps value ownership and consequence-bearing with named humans or institutions.

### 7. Introspection and explanations are not automatically faithful

Human introspection can rationalize; language-model rationales can also be plausible while omitting causal influences. Confidence and explanation must therefore be checked against sources, tools, independent methods, prompt variants, and outcomes. Raw chain-of-thought is not used as proof.

### 8. The right target is complementarity, not imitation

Human cognition has lived context, values, social standing, and consequence; it also has fatigue, limited attention, and bias. AI offers breadth, formalization, retrieval, calculation, repeatability, and systematic variation; it also has grounding, provenance, robustness, and authority limitations. A good workflow allocates operations by comparative advantage and designs a real handoff, rather than making AI imitate human weaknesses or making humans rubber stamps.

## Claim boundary

This synthesis supports workflow controls. It does **not** establish that:

- every human has every listed capacity;
- every AI lacks every listed capacity;
- behavior proves or disproves consciousness;
- biological substrate alone settles intelligence or moral status;
- current architecture fixes all future possibilities.
